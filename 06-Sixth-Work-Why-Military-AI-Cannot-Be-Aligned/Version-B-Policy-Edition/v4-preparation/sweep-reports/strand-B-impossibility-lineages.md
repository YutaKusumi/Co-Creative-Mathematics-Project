## SECTION 1: Adversarial-Examples Inevitability / Concentration of Measure

**1.1 Shafahi, Huang, Studer, Feizi, Goldstein — "Are adversarial examples inevitable?" (ICLR 2019, arXiv:1809.02104)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02104 ; https://davidstutz.de/are-adversarial-examples-inevitable-shafahi-et-al/ (arXiv PDF text extraction failed; details cross-checked via the Stutz review and secondary sources)
- Precise claim: For certain classes of problems, adversarial examples are mathematically inescapable — fundamental bounds on the susceptibility of *any* classifier. Concrete theorems are proved for data on the unit sphere and unit cube (and for sparse vs. dense attacks): with high probability a sample is either misclassified or admits an epsilon-perturbation that is misclassified, and this probability → 1 as dimension grows.
- Assumptions: data supported on the sphere/cube with a density upper bound relative to uniform; isoperimetric inequalities (concentration of measure) on those spaces; result is about *existence* of perturbations, class-agnostic.
- Does NOT claim: that real image distributions satisfy these conditions; that adversarial examples are *findable* efficiently (making them hard to compute is left open as a valid defense); that the bounds are tight for low-dimensional data manifolds.
- Self-declared / reviewer-noted caveats: real data lives on low-dimensional manifolds of the cube/sphere, where the bounds may be loose or inapplicable; more concentrated class distributions are more robust by construction; lower-dimensional data may be less susceptible. The paper's title is a question deliberately — the concentrated-metric-space assumption is where the argument can fail.

**1.2 Mahloujifar, Diochnos, Mahmoody — "The Curse of Concentration in Robust Learning: Evasion and Poisoning Attacks from Concentration of Measure" (AAAI 2019, arXiv:1809.03063)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03063
- Precise claim: If the input metric probability space is concentrated (e.g., a normal Lévy family), then *any* classifier with initial constant error α is vulnerable: an adversary can drive risk from α to ≈1 with perturbations of O(√n) (Hamming distance on product distributions is a worked example). Analogous poisoning result: replacing Õ(√m) training examples (with correct labels) amplifies any non-negligible "bad property" of the learned model to probability ≈1.
- Assumptions: concentrated metric probability space; the α>0 initial-error seed (a *perfect* classifier is not covered — the amplification needs a nonzero-measure error region to inflate).
- Does NOT claim: that real-world data distributions are concentrated in the required sense — the abstract provides no empirical verification; silent on computational feasibility of the attacks.
- Caveat structure: the entire result is conditional on concentration; this is exactly the assumption later found empirically shaky (1.4).
- Structurally important for the project: this is the canonical "nonzero error rate is *amplifiable*, not just present" theorem — the same mathematical skeleton as Wolf et al.'s LLM-side argument, four years earlier, in classification.

**1.3 Fawzi, Fawzi, Fawzi — "Adversarial vulnerability for any classifier" (NeurIPS 2018, arXiv:1802.08686)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08686
- Precise claim: If data is generated by a smooth generative model (x = g(z), smooth/Lipschitz g over a latent space), then *any* classifier — regardless of architecture or training — admits small in-distribution adversarial perturbations, with bounds governed by the generator's smoothness and latent dimensionality; perturbations transfer across classifiers.
- Assumptions: existence of a smooth (small-Lipschitz) generative model for the data; bounds degrade as the Lipschitz constant grows.
- Does NOT claim: that natural data admits a smooth low-Lipschitz generator (this is the known escape hatch: real generators of natural images may have large Lipschitz constants, making the bound vacuous). Abstract-level fetch did not surface explicit self-caveats; the smooth-generative assumption is the acknowledged load-bearing wall in the literature's discussion of this paper.
- Related earlier line: Fawzi, Fawzi & Frossard, "Fundamental limits on adversarial robustness" / "Analysis of classifiers' robustness to adversarial perturbations" (arXiv:1502.02590) — upper bounds on robustness of linear/quadratic classifiers via a class-distinguishability measure (found via search; not separately fetched beyond search summaries).

**1.4 Mahloujifar, Zhang, Mahmoody, Evans — "Empirically Measuring Concentration: Fundamental Limits on Intrinsic Robustness" (NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1905.12202)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12202 ; https://uvasrg.github.io/neurips-2019-empirically-measuring-concentration/
- Precise claim: a method to empirically measure/bound concentration of an actual data distribution from samples, converging to the true value; applied to MNIST/CIFAR under ℓ∞ and ℓ2.
- Key finding (this is the field's own self-correction): "there exists a large gap between the robust error achieved by the best current models and the theoretical limits implied by concentration" — i.e., **concentration of measure does NOT explain observed adversarial vulnerability on real datasets**. Either robust training is far from optimal or other mechanisms dominate.
- Does NOT claim: that intrinsic robustness limits are binding in practice.
- Import for the project: the inevitability lineage contains its own published refutation-of-applicability. Anyone citing lineage 1 as evidence for irreducible misbehavior in real systems must cite this paper against themselves; the territory including its counter-arguments is occupied.

**1.5 Dohmatob — "Classifier-independent Lower-Bounds for Adversarial Robustness" (arXiv:2006.09989)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09989
- Precise claim: via optimal transport, variational formulas for Bayes-optimal adversarial error; explicit classifier-independent lower bounds on adversarial error depending only on the geometry of class-conditional distributions (the optimal attack is an optimal transport plan; computable by bipartite matching).
- Assumptions: known class-conditional distributions; measurable classifiers; distance-based attack model.
- Does NOT claim: anything about practical algorithms, deployed systems, or whether the bounds are nonvacuous for real distributions.
- (Dohmatob's earlier "Generalized No Free Lunch Theorem for Adversarial Robustness," ICML 2019, extends Gilmer et al.'s spheres result to broad distribution classes — surfaced in search context; not separately fetched.)

**1.6 Bhagoji, Cullina, Mittal — "Lower Bounds on Adversarial Robustness from Optimal Transport" (NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1909.12272)**
- Search-verified (proceedings + arXiv listing; not fetched beyond search summary): minimum-transportation-cost between class-conditional distributions determines the minimum 0-1 loss of *any* classifier under neighborhood-constrained perturbation; instantiated for Gaussian data with norm-bounded adversaries; studies the gap between this optimum and robustly trained networks on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10.
- Same shape of claim and same caveat class as 1.5: distribution-level, classifier-independent, but conditioned on knowing/modeling the class-conditional distributions.

## SECTION 2: Undecidability / Computability Limits

**2.1 Alfonseca, Cebrian, Fernández Anta, Coviello, Abeliuk, Rahwan — "Superintelligence Cannot be Contained: Lessons from Computability Theory" (JAIR 70:65–76, 2021; arXiv:1607.00913)**
- Search-verified with multiple sources (JAIR PDF, Semantic Scholar, ResearchGate); the CSER response page returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.
- Precise claim: "total containment" of a superintelligent AI — deciding, for an arbitrary program with superintelligent capability, whether it will harm humans — is incomputable, by reduction to the Halting Problem / via Rice's theorem (any non-trivial semantic property of a Turing machine, e.g. "harms humans," is undecidable).
- Assumptions: the AI and the containment checker are Turing-machine-equivalent; "harm" is a non-trivial semantic property of arbitrary programs; worst-case, exact decision framing.
- Does NOT claim: that *particular* systems can't be verified; that probabilistic or approximate safety assessment is impossible; anything about error *rates* (it is a 0/1 decidability result, not a lower bound on residual misbehavior frequency).
- Known published pushback: CSER response (existence confirmed via search; content unfetchable — 403); and see 2.3.

**2.2 de Melo, Maximo, Soma, de Castro — "On the Undecidability of Artificial Intelligence Alignment: Machines that Halt" (arXiv:2408.08995; journal version "Machines that halt resolve the undecidability of artificial intelligence alignment," Scientific Reports, 2025, s41598-025-99060-2)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08995 (Nature page redirected to an auth wall; journal-version existence confirmed via search result title/URL)
- Precise claim: the *inner alignment* problem — deciding whether an arbitrary AI model satisfies a non-trivial alignment function — is undecidable (Rice's theorem).
- Proposed resolution (this is the occupied "constructive" flank): alignment must be guaranteed *by construction* — build from an enumerable set of provenly-aligned operations plus a halting constraint guaranteeing finite execution; verification-post-hoc is the doomed strategy, construction is not.
- Does NOT claim: alignment is impossible; only that post-hoc verification of arbitrary systems is.
- Caveat: applies to arbitrary models, not architecture-constrained ones.

**2.3 Fox — "On Formally Undecidable Traits of Intelligent Machines" (arXiv:2402.09500)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09500
- Precise claim: **contradicts Alfonseca et al.** — argues Rice's theorem cannot in general be used to conclude that deciding traits like containment/morality of arbitrary machines is impossible, using a formalism mathematically distinct from formal-language theory. Deciding such traits is "not necessarily logically impossible."
- Does NOT claim: that the traits ARE decidable, nor practical verification methods.
- Import: the undecidability sub-lineage is not only occupied, it is *contested terrain* with published counter-arguments; naive citation of "containment is undecidable" is already answerable from within the literature.

**2.4 Panigrahy & Sharan — "Limitations on Safe, Trusted, Artificial General Intelligence" (arXiv:2509.21654, Sept 2025, rev. May 2026)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21654
- Precise claim: under formal definitions — accuracy = never makes false claims (may abstain); trust = the assumption the system is accurate; human-level reasoning = matches/exceeds human capability — an accurate and trusted AI cannot be a human-level reasoner. Proofs parallel Gödel's incompleteness and Turing's halting undecidability.
- Self-declared caveat (explicit): results apply only to the formal definitions; no claim about practical systems or informal senses of the terms; relaxations not addressed.
- Note: this is a 2025–2026 entry — the diagonalization flank is still being actively extended, i.e., not merely historically occupied.

**2.5 Adjacent (search-surfaced, not fetched):** S. Haider, "The Impossibility of AI Containment: Logical, Mathematical..." (philsci-archive 24223); Yampolskiy's uncontrollability corpus (see Section 6).

## SECTION 3: Goodhart's Law Formalizations

**3.1 Manheim & Garrabrant — "Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law" (arXiv:1803.04585)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585
- Precise claim: a taxonomy of (at least) four distinct mechanisms by which metric overoptimization fails — the four variants (regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial; the four names are from the paper/Garrabrant's framework; the fetched abstract confirms "four different mechanisms" without naming them). Explicitly aimed at ML and AI alignment among other domains.
- Assumptions/status: **a conceptual categorization, not a theorem paper** — no formal proofs. Its own framing: existing discussion was ambiguous and the taxonomy clarifies it.
- Does NOT claim: quantitative divergence rates, inevitability, or lower bounds.

**3.2 Skalse, Howe, Krasheninnikov, Krueger — "Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking" (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2209.13085)**
- Search-verified via OpenReview/NeurIPS proceedings/arXiv listings (multiple independent summaries; abstract-level).
- Precise claim (the impossibility core): define a proxy as *unhackable* relative to the true reward if increasing expected proxy return can never decrease expected true return. Theorem: over the set of **all** stochastic policies, no non-trivial unhackable proxy pairs exist — any unhackable, non-trivial pair of reward functions on all stationary policies is equivalent. Non-trivial unhackable pairs exist only when restricting to finite policy sets. Linearity of reward in state-action visit counts makes unhackability an extremely strong condition.
- Assumptions: MDP framework; reward linear in occupancy measures; "hacking" defined via the proxy/true return ordering.
- Does NOT claim: how *much* true return is lost, or at what optimization pressure; it is an existence/equivalence result, not a rate result.
- Caveat (the paper's own): the definition is deliberately strict; weaker notions might admit useful proxies.

**3.3 Zhuang & Hadfield-Menell — "Consequences of Misaligned AI" (NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2102.03896)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03896 (abstract; theorem internals not extractable from abstract page)
- Precise claim: in a resource-constrained principal-agent world with L utility-relevant attributes and a proxy reward supported on only J < L of them, they give **necessary and sufficient conditions under which indefinitely optimizing ANY incomplete proxy drives overall true utility arbitrarily low** (to minus infinity / arbitrarily bad).
- Assumptions: resource constraints coupling attributes (optimizing the mentioned J consumes resources from the omitted L−J); incompleteness J < L; indefinite optimization.
- Mitigations proven within the same paper: full-state-referencing rewards; dynamically updated proxies (interactive reward design); i.e., the paper itself contains the escape conditions.
- Does NOT claim: that proxies are incomplete in practice, or anything about finite-optimization regimes below the divergence threshold.
- This is the closest thing in the lineage to "a theorem that optimizing a proxy provably diverges from the true objective" — that slot is occupied.

**3.4 Karwowski, Hayman, Bai, Kiendlhofer, Griffin, Skalse — "Goodhart's Law in Reinforcement Learning" (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.09144)**
- Search-verified (ICLR proceedings PDF, arXiv, OpenReview listings).
- Precise claim: quantifies Goodharting in MDPs; geometric explanation (occupancy-measure polytope) for why optimizing a proxy beyond a critical point decreases true reward; **provable early-stopping method that avoids the pitfall, with regret bounds**; worst-case reward maximization under reward uncertainty.
- Import: the lineage has moved past "divergence exists" to "divergence is characterized and provably avoidable under conditions" — an occupied *and matured* territory.

**3.5 Adjacent 2025–2026 (search-surfaced, not fetched):** "Inference-Time Reward Hacking in Large Language Models" (arXiv:2506.19248) — best-of-n/inference-time analogues.

## SECTION 4: Impossibility of Safe Large Models

**4.1 El-Mhamdi, Farhadkhani, Guerraoui, Gupta, Hoang, Pinot, Rouault, Stephan — "On the Impossible Safety of Large AI Models" (arXiv:2209.15259)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15259
- Precise claim: systematizes statistical lower-bound results building "a compelling case against the fundamental possibility of building arbitrarily accurate AND secure large models": (a) high accuracy on heterogeneous user-generated data requires memorization → accuracy vs. privacy trade-off; (b) accuracy vs. Byzantine robustness under data poisoning by fake users — with heterogeneous data, a fraction of poisoned contributors imposes an error floor that grows with model dimension ("the poison of dimensionality," follow-up at arXiv:2409.17328); (c) high-dimensional interpolation regime is the mechanism.
- Assumptions: high-dimensional models; heterogeneous multi-source user data; adversarial fraction of contributors; the impossibility is of the *simultaneous* conjunction (arbitrary accuracy + safety), not of safety per se.
- Does NOT claim: a quantified residual *misbehavior rate* for a given trained model; it is about the training-data supply chain and privacy/robustness, not about rule-following under strict training. Abstract's own hedging: "compelling case against," a survey of lower bounds rather than one master theorem.
- Status: arXiv 2022 (v2 2023); a related position paper appears in ICML 2025 proceedings (dl.acm 42nd ICML entry surfaced in search; not separately verified in detail).

**4.2 Tsipras, Santurkar, Engstrom, Turner, Madry — "Robustness May Be at Odds with Accuracy" (ICLR 2019, arXiv:1805.12152)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12152
- Precise claim: robustness-accuracy trade-off **provably exists in a specific constructed distribution** (a simple/natural setting); robust and standard classifiers learn fundamentally different features.
- Does NOT claim: universality across distributions — the theorem is for their construction; broader applicability is empirical. (Later literature — e.g., Yang et al. on separated real data — shows the trade-off is not fundamental for all real datasets; not fetched, noted from search context in 1.x threads.)

**4.3 Safety–helpfulness trade-off, 2024–2026**
- Searched: safety-helpfulness Pareto theory. Finding: the recent literature (Equilibrate RLHF arXiv:2502.11555; BSO arXiv:2605.12339; ASCL arXiv:2602.13562; Safety Game arXiv:2510.09330; LoRA-for-safety arXiv:2507.17075; MultiRisk arXiv:2512.24587) treats safety-vs-helpfulness as an empirical Pareto frontier to be *pushed*, not as a proven impossibility; I found **no theorem of the form "no model can be simultaneously ε-safe and δ-helpful"** in this sweep. Constrained-optimization/lexicographic formulations exist, and "Pareto-optimal trade-offs" language is common, but as engineering framing.
- Honest negative claim, per mandate: my searches (Section 8, queries 12) did not surface a 2024–2026 formal safety-helpfulness impossibility theorem for LLMs. Wolf et al.-adjacent work may contain one (out of my scope). A dedicated search for "no-free-lunch alignment helpfulness theorem" was NOT run — flagging as a residual gap in this sweep.

## SECTION 5: Statistical Detection Limits (rare-misbehavior measurement)

This is the sub-lineage closest to the project's Addendum D finding ("near-floor base rates make intervention effects unmeasurable"). Verdict up front: the *phenomenon* is heavily occupied; a clean *formalized impossibility theorem* in the project's exact form (two-arm intervention comparison at near-floor base rates) is only partially occupied.

**5.1 Jones, Tong, Mu, Mahfoud, Leike, Grosse, Kaplan, Fithian, Perez, Sharma (Anthropic) — "Forecasting Rare Language Model Behaviors" (arXiv:2502.16797, Feb 2025)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16797
- Precise claim: standard LM evaluations **fail to capture risks that emerge only at deployment scale**; elicitation probabilities of target behaviors scale predictably (power-law-like) with query volume, enabling forecasts of worst-query risk across ~3 orders of magnitude of query volume from small evaluation sets; validated on dangerous-chemistry and power-seeking behaviors.
- Assumptions: stability of the elicitation-probability distribution between evaluation and deployment query streams; the scaling regularity itself.
- Import: this paper *states the measurement-limits premise explicitly* (small-n evals cannot see deployment-scale rare failures) and then supplies a partial statistical remedy. The premise is occupied; the remedy is occupied.

**5.2 Kim, Gu, Tiwari, Kolter — "Measuring Five-Nines Reliability: Sample-Efficient LLM Evaluation in Saturated Benchmarks" (arXiv:2605.11209)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11209
- Precise claim: estimating failure rates ~1e-5 with tight confidence requires ~10^7 samples under naive Monte Carlo — computationally infeasible; because LLM failures cluster systematically, a cross-entropy-method importance sampler achieves up to ~156x inference reduction on parameterized GSM8K.
- Does NOT state a formal lower-bound theorem for naive sampling (the binomial-variance argument is treated as standard); the impossibility is practical/economic, not information-theoretic novelty.

**5.3 ARC — Wu et al., "Estimating the Probabilities of Rare Outputs in Language Models" (arXiv:2410.13211; blog: alignment.org)**
- Search-verified (arXiv listing, ARC blog, OpenReview follow-up "Optimizing Importance Sampling Methods for Rare Output Estimation").
- Precise claim: "low probability estimation" — estimating probabilities too small to observe by random sampling — defined as an alignment-relevant problem (motivated by worst-case behavior under distribution shift, deceptive alignment); importance sampling (ITGIS, MHIS) and activation extrapolation both beat naive sampling; minimizing estimated low probabilities generalizes adversarial training.
- Import: the exact conceptual slot "behavioral sampling cannot reach the rates that matter; here are estimation channels beyond behavioral sampling" is occupied by ARC since Oct 2024.

**5.4 Goldwasser, Kim, Vaikuntanathan, Zamir — "Planting Undetectable Backdoors in Machine Learning Models" (FOCS 2022, arXiv:2204.06974)**
- Search-verified (arXiv, TAU CRIS, STOC 2025 follow-up "Oblivious Defense in ML Models: Backdoor Removal without Detection").
- Precise claim: using digital signatures, a malicious trainer can plant a backdoor such that the backdoored model is **computationally indistinguishable** from an honest one; with black-box access, it is computationally infeasible to find even one input where they differ. This is a *cryptographic* (not statistical) impossibility of detection — no polynomial-time auditor, behavioral or otherwise (in one of their two frameworks, even with white-box access), can detect it.
- Assumptions: standard cryptographic hardness; adversarial trainer (the threat model is a malicious learner, not emergent misalignment).
- Import: the strongest known "auditing cannot certify absence of rare misbehavior" result; occupied since 2022.

**5.5 Casper et al. — "Black-Box Access is Insufficient for Rigorous AI Audits" (FAccT 2024, arXiv:2401.14446)**
- Search-verified (ACM DL, FAccT PDF, Apollo Research page).
- Claim: position/argument paper — black-box behavioral evaluation is structurally limited (query-design bias, developer gaming of known tests); white-box and outside-the-box access are needed. Not a theorem; a systematized argument.

**5.6 Jiang, Gjølbye, Zhang, Koyejo — "When Behavioral Safety Evaluation Fails: A Representation-Level Perspective" (arXiv:2606.08044, June 2026)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08044
- Precise claim: formalizes the **"audit gap"** = difference between behavioral safety and robustness under (parameter/latent) intervention; "dissociated models" show safe refusal behavior with substantially elevated Latent Vulnerability Scores; behavioral metrics are insufficient measures of representation-level robustness.
- Import: a 2026 formalization of "behavioral evaluation systematically under-measures" — directly adjacent to the project's measurement-limits point, from the representation side rather than the sample-size side.

**5.7 Miller (Anthropic) — "Adding Error Bars to Evals" (arXiv:2411.00640)**
- Search-verified. Standard-statistics treatment of eval uncertainty (super-population framing, clustered SEs up to 3x naive, paired-difference variance reduction). Relevant occupied ground: the observation that eval comparisons without power analysis are underpowered is mainstream since late 2024.
- Also surfaced: "Noisy but Valid: Robust Statistical Evaluation of LLMs with Imperfect Judges" (arXiv:2601.20913); "Evaluating LLM Safety Under Repeated Inference via Accelerated Prompt Stress Testing" (arXiv:2602.11786). Not fetched.
- Honest residual: I did **not** find a paper that formalizes the project's precise two-arm form — "when the control-arm base rate is already near floor (k≈1–2/30), no realistic n can certify an intervention effect" — as a named theorem. The ingredients (binomial power analysis; Five-Nines' 1e-5→10^7 arithmetic; Forecasting Rare Behaviors' premise) are all published, and any statistician would call the two-arm version elementary (Fisher-exact power). So: not occupied as a *citable theorem*, thoroughly occupied as *known folklore + adjacent formalizations*. A novelty claim here would be weak.

## SECTION 6: Cross-Lineage Syntheses — DO unified "residual risk is irreducible" arguments already exist?

**Yes. At least three, of increasing recency. This materially affects positioning.**

**6.1 Brcic & Yampolskiy — "Impossibility Results in AI: A Survey" (ACM Computing Surveys 56, 2023; arXiv:2109.00484)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00484 (abstract page; PDF text extraction failed, so the per-result inventory below the abstract level is unverified from primary text)
- This is exactly the feared object: a peer-reviewed survey organizing impossibility theorems relevant to AI safety into five mechanism categories — **deduction, indistinguishability, induction, tradeoffs, intractability** — and drawing the synthesis conclusion that "**deductive impossibilities deny 100%-guarantees for security**." It adds new results (unfairness of explainability; misalignment between clones; limits on agent self-awareness) and critically notes that "certain theorems are too specific or have implicit assumptions that limit application" — i.e., it even performs the applicability-caveat audit itself. Given Yampolskiy's co-authorship, coverage of containment-undecidability and unverifiability is near-certain, but I could not verify the exact per-topic inventory (adversarial-inevitability? Goodhart?) from the fetched abstract — flagged as unverified.
- Positioning consequence: any "we synthesize impossibility lineages into an irreducible-residual-risk claim" contribution must position against this survey explicitly.

**6.2 Yampolskiy — "AI: Unexplainable, Unpredictable, Uncontrollable" (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 2024; plus earlier "On the Controllability of AI" JCS 2022)**
- Search-verified (Routledge, Taylor & Francis, reviews). Book-length synthesis applying halting-problem/Rice-theorem-style results plus unpredictability/unexplainability arguments to conclude safety guarantees for AGI are mathematically unreachable and control may be impossible. This is the maximalist version of the unified argument, in monograph form, from one of the survey's authors.

**6.3 Mohsin et al. — "On the Fundamental Limits of LLMs at Scale" (arXiv:2511.12869, Nov 2025, under review TMLR)**
- Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12869
- A "unified, proof-informed framework" explicitly synthesizing **computational undecidability, statistical sample insufficiency, and finite information capacity** into a hierarchy of impossibility and saturation results for LLMs — "diagonalization guarantees inputs on which some model must fail"; "undecidable queries induce infinite failure sets for all computable predictors"; irreducible hallucination baseline from corpus imperfection. Covers five bottlenecks (hallucination, context compression, reasoning degradation, retrieval fragility, multimodal misalignment), pairs theorems with empirics, and proposes mitigations.
- This is a 2025–2026, LLM-specific, multi-lineage synthesis of "some failure is provably irreducible." It does not (per the fetched summary) center *rule-breaking/safety* behavior specifically — its failure objects are correctness/hallucination — but the synthesis *form* is taken.

**6.4 Near-misses also surfaced:** "The Behavioral Credibility Trilemma: When Calibrated Autonomy Becomes Impossible" (arXiv:2605.25739 — trilemma-style impossibility for alignment procedures: representativeness/tractability/robustness mutually exclusive, per search summary; not fetched); "EvalSafetyGap" survey (arXiv:2606.30219). The Kalai & Vempala / OpenAI "Why Language Models Hallucinate" line (irreducible hallucination lower bounds) was not explicitly swept — noting as a residual gap, though 6.3 partially subsumes it.

**Bottom line for Section 6: the unified-synthesis slot is occupied three times over (survey, monograph, LLM-specific framework).** What none of the three does, so far as fetched content shows: connect the synthesis to *empirically measured near-floor residual rates in a preregistered intervention experiment*, or to the two-tier verification/expression architecture. The unoccupied space is the *junction* to primary experimental data, not the synthesis itself.

## SECTION 7: Occupied-Territory Verdict Per Sub-Lineage

1. **Adversarial inevitability / concentration**: **Heavily occupied, including its own published limits.** Existence theorems (any classifier, any nonzero-error classifier), amplification theorems, classifier-independent OT lower bounds, AND the empirical self-refutation (concentration doesn't explain real-data vulnerability). Nothing in the "some misbehavior region is unavoidable and amplifiable, conditional on distribution geometry" space is free. The known open flank: whether real-world *LLM behavior* distributions are "concentrated" in any relevant sense — but note that Wolf et al. (excluded here) is precisely the LLM transplant, so the project cannot claim that flank either.
2. **Undecidability**: **Occupied and contested.** Containment (Alfonseca 2021), inner-alignment verification (de Melo 2024/Sci Rep 2025), general traits (Fox 2024 — *counter*-result), accuracy/trust/reasoning trilemma (Panigrahy & Sharan 2025–26). Any new Rice's-theorem application to alignment verification would be a footnote to this exchange. The literature has also already made the standard move *past* undecidability (aligned-by-construction, halting-constrained architectures). Also, crucially, undecidability results are 0/1 worst-case and do not by themselves yield "nonzero *rate*" claims — a distinction the project should preserve, but Brcic-Yampolskiy's applicability-caveat discussion likely already makes it (unverified from primary text).
3. **Goodhart formalizations**: **Occupied end-to-end**: taxonomy (2018), no-nontrivial-unhackable-proxy theorem (2022), arbitrarily-low-utility divergence theorem with necessary-and-sufficient conditions (2020), geometric characterization plus provable early-stopping remedies (2024), inference-time variants (2025). The lineage even includes its own mitigations. The only nuance: these are reward-optimization results, not "post-training residual rule-breaking rate" results — the mapping between the two is arguable but would itself need defending.
4. **Impossible safety of large models**: **Occupied at the trade-off level, weaker at the theorem level for safety-helpfulness.** El-Mhamdi et al. own "accuracy vs. privacy/poisoning-robustness is impossible at scale"; Tsipras et al. own the constructed robustness-accuracy trade-off (with its narrowness on record). The specific formal claim "safety and helpfulness are provably Pareto-incompatible for LLMs" did not surface in my searches — but the empirical-Pareto framing saturates 2024–2026 applied work, and my sweep here has a flagged gap (no dedicated no-free-lunch-for-alignment search). Treat as "probably partially open, low confidence."
5. **Statistical detection limits**: **Premise occupied, remedies occupied, cryptographic worst case occupied; the project's exact two-arm near-floor formalization not found as a named result.** Forecasting Rare Behaviors (2025) states the small-n-cannot-see-deployment-risk premise in print with Anthropic authorship; Five-Nines (2026) does the sample-complexity arithmetic; ARC (2024) named low-probability estimation as an alignment problem; Goldwasser et al. (2022) prove detection can be *cryptographically* impossible; audit-gap (2026) formalizes behavioral-eval insufficiency. A contribution claiming novelty must be scoped down to: "empirical demonstration, in a preregistered comparative design, that a specific alignment intervention's effect is unmeasurable because a prior intervention already drove base rates to the floor" — the *experience report* and its design consequences, not the statistical fact, which is elementary and effectively folklore.

**Overall adversarial verdict:** the field is NOT open at the level of "impossibility/limitation results imply irreducible residual risk" — that thesis exists as a survey (2023), a monograph (2024), and an LLM-specific unified framework (2025/26), plus five mature sub-lineages each carrying its own caveats and counter-results. Space that genuinely did not surface in this sweep: (a) a formalization tying *training-strictness* to a *lower bound on residual rule-breaking rate* for LLMs — but that is precisely Wolf et al.'s territory (excluded, other agent); (b) the junction of the synthesis to preregistered empirical near-floor measurements; (c) possibly a formal safety-helpfulness impossibility theorem (low confidence, flagged gap).
